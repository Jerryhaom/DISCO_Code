Methods:
For CHARLS, mortality evidence was reconciled across the2013,2015 and2018
follow-up waves. Participants were interval-censored between their last
confirmed alive wave and their first confirmed deceased wave; surviving
participants were right-censored at their last confirmed alive wave rather
than assigned the maximum follow-up. Participants with baseline-only evidence
were excluded. A semiparametric interval-censored proportional-hazards model
was fitted with age, sex, smoking, drinking and education, preserving the
DISCO and log(DM) standard deviations from the original common analysis
population. Covariance was estimated from200 participant bootstrap samples.
In a separate retrospective sensitivity, ten unambiguous Gregorian deaths
before2018 reported in the2020 exit interview were added, while one
death-date/later-alive contradiction was isolated. No precise blood-draw or
individual elapsed death dates were imputed.

Results:
The observed-wave interval analysis included11,370 participants and1,096
deaths. The adjusted HR per original SD was1.461 (95% CI1.381-1.545) for
DISCO and1.435 (1.363-1.511) for log(DM). The strict retrospective analysis
included1,106 deaths and yielded HRs of1.465 (1.383-1.552) and1.439
(1.368-1.513), respectively. The five-cohort pooled HRs changed from1.479
to1.487 for DISCO and from1.354 to1.357 for log(DM) when the strict
CHARLS sensitivity replaced only the CHARLS aggregate. This sensitivity
supports the direction of association but does not certify precise individual
follow-up dates or convert ordinary apparent ROC into time-dependent AUC.

Response concept:
We reconstructed the CHARLS last-alive/death evidence rather than treating
all missing follow-up records as alive through the maximum period. We also
checked the public death-date fields against the official source dictionary
and conducted interval-censored and compatible retrospective-date
sensitivities with fixed original score scales and covariates. Associations
were similar across these analyses. We explicitly distinguish the supported
wave-interval analysis from exact individual follow-up, which cannot be
recovered for every participant from the locally available baseline files.
